# Financial Accounting

## Types of Accounts

In financial accounting, there are 5 main types of accounts:
- **asset** - what you own, which could produce income or which you could sell
- **liability** - what you owe to other people
- **equity** - value of the business owned by the owners or shareholders, or the "residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities."
- **income** - money or things of value you receive after selling products or services, or "increases in assets, or decreases in liabilities, that result in increases in equity, other than those relating to contributions from holders of equity claims"
- **expense** - money you spend to pay for products or services, or "decreases in assets, or increases in liabilities, that result in decreases in equity, other than those relating to distributions to holders of equity claims"

The [Fundamental Accounting Equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting_equation) states that: `assets - liabilities = equity`

Assets, liabilities, and equity represent a point in time. Income and expenses represent flows of money in and out.

The expanded accounting equation can be written as: `assets - liabilities = equity + income - expenses`.

Assets and expenses are increased with debits, decreased with credits.
Liabilities, equity, and income are increased with credits, decreased with debits.

Accounting conventions also always write the debits first, to represent that something is received (debit) before it is given up (credit).
